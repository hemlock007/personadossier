Case File 01 · FCPS Part 1

The Overwhelmed First-Timer

Junior doctor, duty-constrained, sometimes already a repeat examinee.

Validated
Exam Track
FCPS Part 1 — Medicine, Surgery, Gynae & Obs, or Paediatrics
Career Stage
Practicing junior doctor; may be a repeat examinee
Study Window
Fragments between duty shifts
Grounded In
R02 · R03 · R06 · R07 · R10
Goals
  • Pass FCPS Part 1 — for repeat examinees, closing a named weak area first
  • Fit meaningful study into unpredictable, short windows between duty
  • Know exactly what changed in a new curriculum edition before spending revision time re-covering settled material
Behaviors
  • Archive-first habit: clears missed/unattended exams in Archive, then follows a planned topic order — the likely user-level mechanism behind Archive's ~8x higher funnel conversion vs. Batch
  • Onboarding confusion is real but moderated by prior exposure to a similar app — not universal to this persona
  • Exam-batch-only subscribers often skip video/PDF content entirely — a time constraint, not a preference
Frustrations
  • Explanations justify the correct answer but not why the wrong options are wrong
  • Video content occasionally mismatched to its labeled topic
  • ResolvedPDFs delivered via a slow-loading Google Drive link — the origin complaint behind the native download feature since shipped
  • Batch/package confusion still requires a WhatsApp message to admin to resolve
  • No affordable path to add class access onto an exam-only batch — currently all-or-nothing at purchase
"Since we're examinees, there's always a worry about a new edition — did anything actually change, or not?"
— R02, FCPS Part 1 · SBA Booster · Paid
What this persona needs
  • Reliable, accurate topic-tagging on video/content — not just complete, but correctly assigned
  • Explanations covering wrong-answer exclusion logic, not only why the correct answer is correct
  • A lightweight upgrade path from exam-only to exam+class, instead of an all-or-nothing purchase
  • Onboarding that doesn't assume zero prior context for users coming from comparable platforms
Case File 02 · Residency (MD/MS)

The Long-Haul Planner

Multi-year, duty-constrained, and — for one sub-type — benchmarking MHS against a sister platform and two external competitors.

Validated
Exam Track
Residency (MD/MS), often sub-faculty specific
Career Stage
Mid-track, multi-phase, duty-constrained
Notable Sub-type
The Comparator — MHS + sister platform + 2 external competitors
Grounded In
R16 · R18
Goals
  • Complete the syllabus on a timeline that matches their actual exam sitting, not a generic one
  • Tell near-identical batch variants apart before committing
  • Judge class/mentor quality before investing time in it
Behaviors
  • Chooses batch type through deliberate exam-date planning, not passive selection
  • The Comparator sub-type: enrolled across four platforms at once — MHS, Live MCQ (a sister platform under the same parent company, a government-job/BCS exam simulator — not a true competitor), and two genuine external competitors, Genesis and Synapse. He benchmarks MHS against all three, but the Live MCQ comparison is an internal, same-company one, not competitive intelligence
Frustrations
  • Batch category confusion, in the respondent's own words
  • An entire subject (Anatomy) missing from Topic Guru for the Residency track — a content-completeness gap, not just a naming issue
  • Repeated/duplicate questions — the single most irritating issue reported in this segment
  • No indicator for which classes are newly added
  • No per-class rating or comment system — modeled directly on Live MCQ (sister platform, same parent company), not a competitor guess
  • WhatsApp support is slow to respond, unlike Live MCQ's team — again, a same-company comparison
  • Price is the acknowledged, dominant reason people choose MHS — not perceived service quality
"Everyone comes to Medical Higher Study because it's cheap — that's the only reason, not because of the service."
— R18, Residency (MD/MS) + multi-package · Paid
What this persona needs
  • Batch differentiation that states what phase/timeline each variant actually serves
  • Content-completeness audits per subject per track — not assumed from naming alone
  • A "New" indicator on recently added classes
  • A rating/comment layer on classes
  • A support model that doesn't rely on a single overloaded WhatsApp group
Case File 03 · Diploma

The Loyal Value-Seeker

Government cadre doctor, no library access, dual user of two products from the same parent company.

Validated
Exam Track
Diploma, surgical faculty orientation
Career Stage
Government cadre doctor — no library access at all
Platform History
Dual user of Live MCQ (since 2019, sister platform) and MHS concurrently; refers candidates to both
Grounded In
R28
Goals
  • Get consolidated, fair value from a subscription — annual and cross-batch, not fragmented into 6-month batch purchases
  • See enough exam participation from other users to stay motivated — social proof and competition matter here as much as personal discipline
  • Content reflecting the real overlap in Diploma/MD/MS/FCPS preparation for surgical and Gynae & Obs faculties
Behaviors
  • Originally hooked into Live MCQ via free weekly model tests — installed, tested free, saw a low score, then purchased
  • High-advocacy: refers other doctors to whichever product he trusts — historically Live MCQ (transcript-evidenced below), and MHS itself, per direct research-team context rather than a transcript quote Team Context
  • Unprompted, detailed structural comparison of MHS's pricing and packaging against Live MCQ's — credible precisely because it's a working sibling product he's used for years, not a guess about an outside competitor's internals
Frustrations
  • Pays roughly double, for six months, what they paid annually elsewhere
  • No free/trial exam entry point — unlike Live MCQ, and plausibly connected to the very low exam participation this respondent observes
  • Fragmented, itemized billing feels bad independent of total cost
  • Question Bank scoped to MD/MS/Diploma only — no FCPS-specific compiled question sets
  • Same-parent-company consistency gap: this isn't an unreasonable external benchmark — Live MCQ and MHS share a parent company, so the comparison is a legitimate consistency expectation, not anchoring bias. This raises the stakes on the asks above: annual pricing, a free-trial exam, and a rating system aren't hypothetical competitor features, they're patterns the parent company has already built and proven (Live MCQ has a documented BCS success record, per R18 in Case File 02) and simply hasn't carried over to MHS yet
"It's like a corporate hospital — one charge for the injection, another charge tacked on separately. Don't split the billing that many times; it's what catches a user's eye."
— R28, Diploma-enrolled · Paid
What this persona needs
  • An annual, cross-batch subscription option — at minimum for faculties where content genuinely overlaps across tracks
  • A consolidated package structure instead of granular paid add-ons (e.g. a separate Question Bank purchase)
  • A free or low-friction trial exam to rebuild the competitive/social-proof loop
  • Onboarding or positioning messaging that makes the parent company's cross-product consistency intentional, rather than something users only notice when it's missing
Case File 04 · Cross-Cutting · Rebuilt

The Advocate

Already paying, already referring others — the risk is losing an advocate, not losing a customer.

Validated
This persona replaces "The Undecided Free User." That earlier version was Hypothesis-status with zero direct evidence — built around a conversion framing the research never actually supported (100% of respondents interviewed are already paying). Under the corrected scope — retention and experience quality for an already-paying base — the relevant cross-cutting persona isn't the unconverted user, it's the highly engaged, loyal advocate: real, evidenced, and directly at risk if friction persists.
Exam Track
Any — spans Residency (R18) and Diploma (R28) at minimum
Stage
Already paying, highly engaged, actively referring others
Defining Trait
Long-form unprompted feedback; proactive referrals; benchmarks MHS against the best available reference
Grounded In
R18 · R28
Goals
  • Wants MHS to actually reach its potential — invested in its success, not just their own personal outcome
  • Wants their advocacy to reflect well on them — if MHS underdelivers for someone they personally referred, that's a credibility cost to them, not just a product complaint Directional
  • Wants a channel for detailed feedback to visibly lead somewhere, not just be absorbed Directional
Behaviors
  • Refers candidates proactively — R28 estimates 30–40 referrals to Live MCQ historically, and to MHS itself per direct research-team context Team Context (MHS referrals)
  • Delivers long-form, structured, unprompted feedback rather than general complaints — both source calls ran long specifically because these respondents had this much to say
  • Frames criticism generously — "room for improvement," not "problems"
  • Extends real-relationship gestures beyond the feedback call itself
Frustrations
  • Feedback loop feels one-directional — neither respondent showed clear evidence that previously-raised issues (e.g. R16's Anatomy content gap, same track as R18) had visibly been addressed Directional
  • Frustrated when the product doesn't match the standard they're implicitly vouching for when they refer someone else in Directional
  • Concrete, specific asks (rating system, annual pricing, consolidated billing) risk being treated as one-off feedback rather than prioritized as the low-risk, high-signal input they actually are Directional
"I won't call these problems — I'll just say there's room for improvement. Issues exist everywhere."
— R18, Residency (MD/MS) + multi-package · Paid
What this persona needs
  • A visible signal that feedback leads to change — even a simple changelog or roadmap note — turns advocacy into a durable asset instead of something quietly spent down
  • Prioritization of the concrete, already-proven asks this persona type tends to bring (rating system, structural pricing fixes)
  • A real relationship channel, not just a feedback form — both respondents offered something closer to free consulting than a complaint

Onboarding & Batch Selection, Unified

Study Routine — Protect & Extend
Tier 2
Current Pain — Validated
Already positive for FCPS (Topic Guru); flat-to-missing for Residency (Anatomy absent entirely)

Stage 3
Content You Can Trust
Tier 1 — highest leverage
Current Pain — Validated
The single worst moment in the entire dataset: repeated/duplicate questions described as making people "short-tempered," plus video-topic mismatches and explanations that justify the right answer but not why the others are wrong
Recommended Fix — Hypothesis
Full tagging QA pass across Video/PDF/Topic Guru; deduplication logic in question serving so the same item doesn't resurface immediately; extend the explanation format to cover why each distractor is wrong, not only why the correct answer is right
Stage 4
Getting Help, Self-Serve First
Tier 1 — proven in-house
Current Pain — Validated

Recommended Fix — Hypothesis
Self-service batch/package clarity (Stage 1 fix reduces this need directly); a per-class rating + comment system — not speculative, it's a pattern Live MCQ (same parent company) has already built and proven, so the implementation risk is organizational, not technical; a dedicated support-responder role
Stage 5
Growing With MHS
Tier 1–3, mixed
Current Pain — Validated
No affordable path from exam-only to exam+class; fragmented, itemized billing feels bad regardless of total cost; no free/trial exam to build the competitive motivation that keeps enrolled users engaged
Recommended Fix — Hypothesis
A lightweight upgrade path at natural renewal moments; a consolidated, annual, cross-batch package (at minimum for faculties where content genuinely overlaps); a free weekly trial exam — again, mirroring a pattern Live MCQ already proved works, not a novel bet

Cheapest, highest leverage
Current Pain — Directional
High-advocacy users show no clear evidence that previously-raised issues get resolved — the feedback loop feels one-directional, which risks the advocacy relationship itself, not just satisfaction Directional finding

Open gaps
Scope correction carried through this dossier: earlier versions of this research treated free-tier, non-converted users as the top interview priority, built around a conversion framing the evidence never actually supported (every respondent interviewed is already paying). Under the corrected retention/advocacy scope, that gap is deprioritized, not urgent — the more relevant open question is whether the feedback-loop visibility gap above holds up across a broader sample of paying users, not just R16/R18/R28.
